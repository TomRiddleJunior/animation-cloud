# Formats and script patterns

Time tables are shares of the length, so they scale. Each format names the engines it usually needs.

## Advert (6, 15 or 30 s)

| Share of length | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0 to 10% | Hook: the problem or the desire, in a picture, in the first second |
| 10 to 60% | The product doing one thing, shown not described |
| 60 to 80% | The benefit, in five words or fewer |
| 80 to 100% | Logo, call to action, URL, held at least 2 s |

One product, one benefit, one action. It works muted: the text on screen carries the message.

A 6-second bumper is one picture and one line, then the logo. A 15 is the table above with a single proof beat. A 30 has room for a problem and two proofs.

## Product ad with 3D inserts (15 to 30 s)

The white-stage fintech pattern.

| Time (20 s) | Beat | Engine |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 3 | The problem as a headline arriving word by word, with objects that illustrate it | 2D type, 3D or 2.5D objects |
| 3 to 5 | The turn, two or three words | 2D type |
| 5 to 8 | Where the product lives (a dock, a home screen, a desk), the cursor choosing it | 3D or generated plate, 2D interface |
| 8 to 15 | The product doing the thing: type, send, confirm, result | 2D interface, close-ups |
| 15 to 18 | The promise as a headline with a signature 3D flourish | 2D type, 3D objects on transparent film |
| 18 to 20 | Logo, held | 2D |

Ration the 3D: two or three inserts carry the whole film.

## Marketing or launch film (30 to 75 s)

Situation, turn, resolution.

1. A person and a specific situation (a seller who just won a large order).
2. The friction, concrete and small (paid in 60 days; the supplier wants paying now).
3. The product enters at the turn and changes one thing.
4. Three feature beats of 6 to 10 s: one claim, one proof on screen.
5. The new situation, shown.
6. Close: name, line, call to action, held 2 to 3 s.

Open on a line a person would say, not a slogan. Show the product by about 0:09 and name it after it has been seen.

## Feature release or changelog (20 to 60 s)

What changed, shown on the real interface. One feature per film where possible. Structure in `product-demo.md`.

## Educational explainer (60 to 180 s)

- Hook (5 to 10 s): a real question or a quoted real post.
- Numbered chapters (01 The problem, 02 The gap ...). Each opens with its title for 1 s.
- One running example with consistent figures across chapters, labelled as illustrative.
- Diagrams where every line is a request, a payment or a permission. No decorative connectors.
- Recap: the whole loop in one frame.
- Close.

## Tutorial

Real captures for actions that need proof, authored scenes for ideas a recording cannot show. Zoom into the part of the screen that matters; a full desktop at 1080p cannot be read.

## Event sizzle (20 to 60 s)

Arresting shot or line, scale, people, substance, peak, calm close with name and date. Music-led. Details in `live-action-edit.md`.

## Testimonial or interview cut (30 to 90 s)

The person's real words, in context, with their release on file. Open on their strongest sentence. Cover edits with relevant cutaways. Name and role as a lower third for 3 s. Captions always.

## Ranked list or compilation (1 to 15 minutes)

- Cold open: the three best seconds of the whole film.
- Title card.
- Segments counting down, each with a number card (1 to 1.5 s), a lower third with the title, the clip opening on its most active second, and a consistent length.
- Recap of the list in one frame.
- End card.

Every clip is licensed or permitted for this use.

## Game or app trailer (15 to 60 s)

Gameplay or app capture is the proof; authored title cards carry the claims. Fast middle, clear ending with the name, the platforms and the date. Store previews have their own rules (`delivery.md`).

## Brand or manifesto film (30 to 90 s)

Voice or type leads. One idea per line, pictures that add to the words instead of repeating them. Kinetic type, mixed media or live action. The logo appears once, at the end.

## Hero loop for a web page (6 to 12 s)

Silent, seamless, small. The last frame equals the first. One slow idea; nothing that demands reading. A poster frame that works alone.

## Writing for the screen

- One idea per line; under 9 words on screen.
- Specific nouns and numbers ("12 rolls of branded tape, Lagos delivery") beat categories ("physical goods").
- No hype words (revolutionary, seamless, game-changing). If the picture shows it, the text does not need to say it.
- Check every word on screen for spelling; a typo in a headline is the most expensive kind.
- Label honestly and visibly: "Illustration", "Example figures", "Planned", "Testnet", "Dramatisation". A label the viewer cannot read does not count.
