## 1. Identity

A long, patient, single-take explanation of **how a system works**, told entirely with **ISOTYPE/AIGA-style
pictograms**: cream silhouette people with detached round heads, houses, office towers, money piles and boxes.
Everything sits on **one radial-vignetted green field** that never changes for 11 minutes. Each abstraction is
given a **physical proxy**: a mortgage is a little house card, a pooled security is a cardboard box cut into
three slices and later three cascading trays, risk is a ticking bomb passed hand to hand. The system is drawn as
a **flow chain** (actor — thick arrow bar — actor) that grows clause by clause, reverses, and finally closes into
a loop. **Disembodied white cartoon-glove hands** on long cream arms reach in from the frame edges to point, hand
over objects and hold signs. Key terms **slam in as heavy varsity/slab capitals** ("FIA$CO!", "CREDIT"); a white
thought-cloud in the top-left names each chapter. Things enter **out of focus and sharpen**. Scenes change with
whip-pans, pull-backs, smoke puffs and starburst explosions, never with cuts.

**Hook pattern (first 15 s):** title, then rapid questions answered in one word each. A jargon list is named
precisely so the film can promise to decode it. No character appears until 29 s.

**How sections hand over:** "Here's how it works" plus a new chapter cloud; recurring sentence templates ("X calls
up Y", "connects X to Y", "makes a nice commission"); the same proxies everywhere, so the viewer learns the cast
once. Numbers are always worked through on screen.

**Ending:** no CTA. The loop closes, the title phrase returns as the last line, credits follow. The film's
argument is the diagram itself.

Rules: **one field colour + cream + white**; every pictogram uses the same cream; red, blue and other hues never
appear. The palette's high saturation (0.595) comes from the green field alone.

### Pictogram construction
- **People:** AIGA/ISOTYPE-like. The circular head floats detached above the shoulders (head diameter ≈ 0.16 of
  figure height). Torso and limbs are rounded slabs with no outline, no face and no hands beyond rounded ends.
  Gender comes from the dress silhouette, children from scale (≈ 0.45×) and a raised-arms pose; the family dog is
  a recurring cast member. A featured figure is ≈ 35 % of frame height (a man ≈ 375 px at 1080p); crowds shrink
  to 4–6 %. Caricature variants (fat banker, slouching subprime borrower) are the same kit with altered
  silhouettes.
- **Buildings and props:** office towers as tall slabs with a window-grid stripe texture, banks with columns,
  houses as a pentagon with a 2 × 2 window, money as stepped pyramids marked "$", cardboard boxes as cream cubes
  with green labels, signposts, FOR SALE paddles, a round bomb with a fuse, a wizard banker with a wand.
- **Connectors:** thick rounded bars (10–14 px at 1080p) in cream on green or green on white, with arrowheads,
  drawn between actors; dotted thought-bubble trails.

### Line work, texture, lighting, depth
No strokes on figures; shapes are fills only. Each pictogram carries a slight top-to-bottom gradient and soft
ground contact. **Depth of field is a motion device**: entering elements start blurred (≈ 12 px at 1080p) and
sharpen over ≈ 9 f; exits blur out. Explosions bloom white-yellow. There is no grain, though the 2009 master is
soft.

### Composition
The main actor group sits in the bright centre third. Chains run horizontally across 55–80 % of the width at
45–55 % H. Chapter clouds live top-left; tiny "narrator" figures with thought trails sit in the top-right corner.
Glove arms enter from the nearest edge, usually bottom-right or the left edge. Dense vignettes alternate with
near-empty frames holding one word.

| chapter clouds, speech bubbles | bold condensed sans caps, green on white | Barlow Condensed Bold / Oswald 600 | 36–48 px |

Text density is low: key nouns, numbers and chapter names only, about one new text element every 10–20 s, except
for the type-slam passages.

## 6. Characters & objects

- **Acting is staging, not animation.** Figures pop, slide, swap pose and scale; arms barely bend. Emotion is
  carried by props (a sign, a bomb, a money pile shrinking), by scale (a banker grows fat with profit) and by the
  narrator's wry asides ("financial magic", "banker wizards").
- **Glove hands** are the transaction device: a long cream arm with a white four-finger glove slides in over
  8–12 f to point, hand over a box, hold a sign ("NORMAL DEAL") or receive money; it exits the same way.
- **Caricature swap:** to show a change in kind (prime → subprime), the family members drop out and variant
  silhouettes drop in from above (6 f vertical swap with blur).
- **The bomb:** once introduced, it is the single prop the whole collapse act follows.
- Design every proxy so it can be counted, stacked, sliced, passed or exploded. Those are the only verbs the film
  needs.

## 8. Motion grammar

- **Easing:** power2/power3.out slides; back.out(1.5–2) for pops (≈ 10 % overshoot); expo.in for whips, dives and
  type slams; sine for smoke. Blur is part of most moves (motion blur on whips, depth blur on entrances).
- **Stagger:** crowds and house rows pop ≈ 5 f apart (six houses in ≈ 1 s); chain connectors 6–8 f each; type-on
  letters about 1 per frame.
- **Build-on order:** actor → label or tag → connector → travelling object → consequence (pile grows or shrinks).
  Then **hold** while the narration finishes the clause.
- **Rhythm against narration:** each build lands on the noun or number being spoken; a slam word lands on the
  stressed syllable; whips fall on sentence boundaries that start a new idea; holds fill the rest. Worked
  examples move in lock-step with the arithmetic.
- **Energy arc:** a lively hook, a type-slam spike at the first mechanism (decile 2), a long calm build-up, then a
  rising tail of explosions and smoke in the collapse.

## 10. Do / Don't

**Do**
1. Keep one vignetted field colour for the whole film; let cream pictograms and white bubbles carry everything else.
2. Build the cast once: every actor group gets one pictogram and every abstraction one physical proxy.
3. Draw the system as a flow chain that grows clause by clause and finally closes into a loop.
4. Work every number through on screen with tags that update in place.
5. Use glove hands on long arms for every hand-over, point and sign.
6. Bring things in out of focus and sharpen them over about 9 frames.
7. Name each chapter in a persistent thought-cloud at top-left.
8. Slam key terms in heavy slab capitals, one word per stressed syllable.
9. Change topic with a whip-pan, a pull-back, a smoke puff or an explosion; hold 1–3 s after each build.
10. Use one inverted white-ground segment for the overview or the scheme, and one dark segment for the collapse.
11. Open with a question cascade answered in one word each, and end by closing the loop on the title phrase.
12. Keep the narration plain and slow (120–135 WPM) with the music never stopping.

**Don't**
1. Don't cut; the film is one take.
2. Don't add faces, outlines or a second figure colour; pictograms stay anonymous cream silhouettes.
3. Don't introduce new hues; the palette is field + cream + white (+ black, + explosion yellow).
4. Don't show an abstraction as text alone when it can be a box, a slice, a tray or a bomb.
5. Don't animate limbs naturalistically; stage with pops, slides, swaps and scale.
6. Don't put more than one sentence of text on screen; labels and numbers only.
7. Don't change the proxy for a concept midway.
8. Don't rush the holds; the viewer needs 1–3 s to read each new state.
9. Don't use stock icons that clash with the rounded, detached-head pictogram family.
10. Don't add a CTA or logo sting; the closed loop is the ending.
11. Don't copy the original's crest, its exact proxies' artwork or its title lettering; build an original kit.
12. Don't let explosions or smoke become decoration; reserve them for scene changes and the collapse.

## 11. Replication recipe (code)

**Rendering:** SVG in the engine at 1920×1080, 30 fps. Use the `doc-pulse` or a custom downtempo beat preset.
Rasterise the field once (`<image href>` of a radial gradient) for speed.

**Layer stack:** `field` (radial gradient rect: centre #84bb2e at 50 %/45 %, r = 75 %; stops #84bb2e 0 %,
#6d9a27 45 %, #486520 80 %, #3d531c 100 %) → `cam` group (chain row, actors, props) → `bubbles` (screen space:
chapter cloud, speech bubbles, tags) → `type` (slam words) → `fx` (smoke clouds, starbursts) → optional soft
blur pass for entrances.

**Pictogram kit:** `<symbol>`s for man, woman, child, dog, banker (fat), broker (suit), house, tower, bank, money
pile, box, slice, tray, bomb, sign and glove arm. All are cream with `fill:url(#creamGrad)` (#ede6c0 → #c9c37e
top to bottom) and no stroke; the head is a separate circle with a 6–8 px gap above the shoulders.

```js
const FPS = 30, F = n => n / FPS;
function focusIn(el, at) {                       // signature entrance
  X.tl.fromTo(el, { opacity: 0, filter: "blur(12px)" }, { opacity: 1, filter: "blur(0px)", duration: F(9), ease: "power2.out" }, at);
}
function chain(nodes, at, step = F(8)) {          // T13
  nodes.slice(1).forEach((n, i) => {
    const bar = connector(nodes[i], n);           // rounded 12 px bar + arrowhead
    X.tl.fromTo(bar, { attr: { x2: bar.x1 } }, { attr: { x2: bar.x2 }, duration: step, ease: "power2.inOut" }, at + i * step);
  });
}
function slam(words, times) {                      // T3, times from vo.json stressed words
  words.forEach((w, i) => X.tl.fromTo(w, { scale: 8, opacity: 0, filter: "blur(10px)" },
    { scale: 1, opacity: 1, filter: "blur(0px)", duration: F(4), ease: "expo.in", transformOrigin: "50% 50%" }, times[i] - F(4)));
}
function whip(outGroup, inGroup, at) {             // T5
  X.tl.to(outGroup, { x: "-=1920", duration: F(4), ease: "power3.in" }, at);
  focusIn(inGroup, at + F(4)); X.sfx(at, "whoosh");
}
function smokeMagic(target, swap, at) {            // T8: 30 seeded circles growing from behind the target
  const puffs = cloud(target, 30, X.rand(3));
  X.tl.fromTo(puffs, { scale: 0 }, { scale: 1, duration: F(15), stagger: F(0.5), ease: "sine.out" }, at)
      .add(() => swap(), at + F(15))
      .to(puffs, { opacity: 0, scale: 1.3, duration: F(20), ease: "sine.in" }, at + F(18));
}
```

**Defining shots and how to build them**
1. **Cast introduction:** two pictogram groups left and right of centre, each with its proxy popping beside it
   (house cards, a money pile), a signpost pair between them, glove arms bringing in the signs. Focus-in entrances,
   5 f stagger.
2. **The flow chain:** 4–6 actors in a row at 50 % H with connectors growing per clause; an object (a house card)
   travels the chain on each "sells it to"; the chapter cloud stays top-left.
3. **The worked example:** one figure with a money tag, a "NORMAL DEAL" sign held by a glove hand, a box bought and
   sold; then the leveraged version with 100 boxes in a grid and tags updating to the spoken figures.
4. **Box → slices → trays:** a smoke-puff transformation slices a box into three labelled layers, which become
   three cascading trays with cream "money" spilling from the top tray down.
5. **Hot potato and collapse:** a bomb with a flickering fuse passes along the chain; FOR SALE signs pop in rows;
   tags drop ($300,000 → $90,000); the field goes dark; starbursts; the chain closes into a loop and the title line
   returns.
6. **Type slam:** a three-line headline built word by word from camera depth over a clean field, then a whip-pan
   out.

## 12. Per-video notes

**The Crisis of Credit Visualized (11 min 10 s)**
- Zero hard cuts in 670 s. It is the longest single-take explainer in the corpus.
- The green field never changes except for one 24 s white segment (the lenders' "idea") and the dark ending.
- The heaviest motion comes from the type passages (decile 2), not from the pictograms.
